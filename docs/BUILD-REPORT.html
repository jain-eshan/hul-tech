One place I could not follow the spec, and why

§11.13 asks for progressive disclosure: deterministic findings at roughly 400ms, judgment findings streaming over two to three seconds, so the architecture is visible to a judge. I built the staged reveal as specified.

But the reason the spec gives for it does not hold here. Our engine returns in single-digit milliseconds, so there is nothing to stream — the reveal is a presentation of a result that was already complete. I kept it because it does make the deterministic and judgment split legible, which is the real point, but you should know that the timing is a deliberate pause, not a measurement.

The genuinely honest version of the same argument is already on every verdict: the measured deterministic and judgment milliseconds, shown separately. That number is real. If a judge asks why clearance takes 90 seconds in the PRD and 3 milliseconds on screen, the answer is that the prototype does no multimodal extraction — the claims are cached, exactly as the replay architecture intends.

Risks I would flag

  • Nothing is deployed and nothing is recorded. This is the largest gap and both are cheap. The single-file build partly covers it — it runs from a laptop with the wifi off, which is a stronger mitigation than a video.
  • The accuracy card reads 100%. A sharp judge will push on that. The screen answers honestly before they ask, but rehearse the answer: thirty cases, author-written labels, enough to catch regressions and not enough to establish production accuracy.
  • Three of nine screens use simulated or seeded data — Creator Sweep fixtures, the Moment Risk composite, and the portfolio reach figures. Each is labelled in the UI. §10.2's scope-honesty rule is the right instinct and it is worth saying these aloud rather than waiting to be asked.
  • PULSE and MEMORY are absent from a product pitched as five agents. If a judge counts, they will notice. The answer is prioritisation discipline, which §1.4 already arms you for — but have it ready.
